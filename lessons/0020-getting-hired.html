Getting hired: target list, resume, interviews

The goal is a job, not a certificate. This lesson turns what you've built into applications: which roles to aim for first, what the top of your resume says, how many applications a week, and ten interview questions to rehearse.

≈ 60 min, then ongoinga spreadsheet to track applications5 a week for 6 weeks

Part 1 · What entry-level job ads tend to ask for

Read 30–50 real ads in your area before you apply anywhere, and note what each asks for. Common patterns:

  • Cloud/DevOps: almost always 1+ years of experience, but many accept “1–2 years in cloud support OR related IT”. So the usual route is 1–2 years in a bridge job, then move.
  • Data center operations: power (UPS, generators, transformers), cooling, reading technical drawings, and willingness to work 24x7 shifts. Electrical and facilities backgrounds fit well.
  • NOC / network: often the most open to fresh entrants. Ads ask for TCP/IP, subnetting, DNS/DHCP, VLANs, routing, monitoring and ticketing, and the CCNA is usually the most-named certificate.
  • ELV / structured cabling / low-voltage systems: CCTV, access control, fire alarm and cabling design. Plan-reading and estimating experience is a plus here.

Treat what you find as direction, not precision: ads differ by city, company size and month.

Part 2 · Your target list, in priority order

  1. NOC / network engineer or technician at telcos, ISPs and managed IT providers. Apply with “CCNA in progress” once Lessons 10–13 and 15 pass. The CCNA itself turns callbacks into offers.
  2. Data center operations (NOC or facilities side): networking plus power, cooling and drawings. Aim for titles that match your background, and check whether the ad requires a trade certificate.
  3. ELV / low-voltage systems: design and install CCTV, access control and cabling. A good side door if you come from electrical or construction work.

Also check career pages directly: telcos, ISPs, data center operators, managed service providers, and the engineering firms and system integrators that design and build data centers.

Part 3 · Translate your experience

Only claim what's true. Headline (top of the resume): “<your current title or licence> · <your degree or certificates> · Network & data center operations (CCNA in progress)”. Recruiters scan the top line first.

Previous-job languageNetwork / data center language
Read plansInterpret single-line diagrams, panel schedules and structured-cabling drawings
Quantity takeoffs, BOQBills of quantities and cost estimates for N projects (numbers make it real)
Fixed things on siteTroubleshot faults step by step, documented the fix, escalated when needed
Customer serviceHandled tickets and kept users updated until the issue was resolved

Projects section: your own server or home lab (Lesson 09 pitch), the capstone network design (Lesson 19), S3 backups + Terraform VPC (Lessons 16–17), a scripts repo (Lesson 18), the ACL + hardening lab (Lesson 21). Certs: list in-progress certs with a target month only if you've booked the exam or are actively studying, e.g. “CCNA (in progress, exam booked)”. AI honesty (Lesson 09): “I built it with AI help, then learned every part. Ask me anything about it.”

Part 4 · Application rhythm

  • 5 targeted applications per week for 6 weeks = 30. Log every one in a spreadsheet the same day: company, role, date, link, status.
  • Every second week, review the numbers: fewer than 10% callbacks after 30 applications → change the resume or the job family, not just study more · callbacks but no interviews → the phone screen is the issue: practise a 20-second pitch · interviews but no offers → mock interviews on the questions below until 8 out of 10 are smooth.
