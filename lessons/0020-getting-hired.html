Getting hired: target list, resume, interviews

The goal is a job, not a certificate. This lesson turns the evidence sheet into action: who to apply to, what the top of your resume says, how many applications a week, and the ten questions to rehearse.

≈ 60 min, then ongoingevidence sheet: Application Tracker, Readiness Checks5 a week for 6 weeks

Part 1 · What the ads told us

October 2026, 68 ads, Metro Manila + remote.

  • Cloud/DevOps: of the ads that state experience, almost all ask for 1+ years. The only 0-year one was a software-developer role. But some accept “1–2 years cloud support OR related IT”. So the plan is right: 1–2 years in a bridge job, then move.
  • Data center: ads ask for exactly what electricians know: UPS, generators, transformers, cooling, technical drawings, RME/NCII. Median listed pay is around ₱30k–₱40k a month.
  • NOC/network: the most open to fresh entrants, but often asks for a degree (engineering/IT) and CCNA. CCNA was the most-named cert. You already have the degree and a licence whose legal scope includes networking.
  • ELV/estimator: data center and mission-critical estimating roles exist, some remote, at ₱50k–₱90k a month. That's a side door that keeps your pay.
  • Honest limits: ads came from search-result summaries, not full postings; counts undercount; snapshot dates vary. Treat the numbers as direction, not precision.

Part 2 · Your target list, in priority order

  1. NOC / network engineer at telcos, ISPs and IT providers (Job Ads #24, #31, #33–36, #62–66, #68). A degree in engineering is the filter that blocks most career shifters; it doesn't block you. Apply with “CCNA in progress” once Lessons 10–13 and 15 pass. The CCNA itself turns callbacks into offers.
  2. Data center NOC / operations ENGINEER (Job Ads #6, #7, #67): electronics + electrical drawings + NOC skills. Aim for engineer titles, not technician roles that ask for RME/NCII (#1).
  3. ELV / auxiliary-systems engineer (Job Ads #60, #61): your licence is the stated requirement, and your takeoff experience is a bonus. This is also the long-term ladder toward data centre telecom engineering (#17) and, later, PECE.

Also check company career pages directly: PLDT / ePLDT VITRO, Globe, Converge, ST Telemedia GDC, Eastern Communications, KMC, CBRE, and the MEP consultancies and system integrators that design and build data centers.

Part 3 · Translate your experience

Only claim what's true. Headline (top of the resume): “<your licence> · <your degree> · Network & data center operations (CCNA in progress)”. Recruiters scan the top line first.

Estimator languageData center / network language
Electrical takeoffsQuantity takeoffs and estimates for power distribution: panelboards, transformers, UPS feeders, generator connections (if those were in your projects)
Read plansInterpret single-line diagrams, panel schedules and ELV/structured-cabling drawings
BOQBills of quantities and cost estimates for N projects worth ₱X total (numbers make it real)

Projects section: Palhaven (Lesson 09 pitch), capstone network design (Lesson 19), S3 backups + Terraform VPC (Lessons 16–17), palhaven-ops repo (Lesson 18), ACL + hardening lab (Lesson 21). Certs: list in-progress certs with a target month only if you've booked the exam or are actively studying, e.g. “CCNA (in progress, exam booked Feb 2027)”. AI honesty (Lesson 09): “I built it with AI help, then learned every part. Ask me anything about it.”

Part 4 · Application rhythm

  • 5 targeted applications per week for 6 weeks = 30. Log every one in the Application Tracker the same day.
  • Every second Sunday, review the Readiness Checks tab with me: fewer than 10% callbacks after 30 applications → change the resume or the job family, not just study more · callbacks but no interviews → the phone screen is the issue: practise the 20-second pitch · interviews but no offers → mock interviews on the questions below until 8 out of 10 are smooth.
