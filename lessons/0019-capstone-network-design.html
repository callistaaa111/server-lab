Capstone: from your takeoff to a network design

This is your interview story no IT graduate can tell. You'll take one building you've estimated and design its network: drops, telecom rooms, switches, PoE, VLANs and IPs, Packet Tracer, rack and UPS, and a bill of quantities.

≈ 4–6 h over a weekLessons 10–15CCNA 1.0–3.0 · Network+ 1.0–2.0Excel/Sheets, Packet Tracer, diagrams.net
Why this lesson

It joins your estimating skill (Job Ads #16, #21–23) with data center and network skills (Lessons 10–15). It works for data center, NOC, network and ELV estimator applications.

Part 1 · Pick the building and protect your employer

Choose one project you've estimated: an office floor, a small mall wing or a school. Before anything leaves your laptop, remove client names, addresses, logos and prices. Call it “Project A, 3-storey commercial building”. Your employer's drawings are not yours to publish. The design you produce from them is your own work.

Part 2 · Deliverables

  • Device count table: data drops, Wi-Fi access points, CCTV cameras, access-control readers, IP phones, printers, per floor. (You do takeoffs: this part is fast for you.)
  • Telecom rooms: where the MDF (main room) and IDFs (floor rooms) go. Check: no cable run longer than 90 m (Lesson 14). If a drop is further, you need another IDF.
  • Switch ports: count ports per room, add 20% spare, choose switch sizes (24 or 48 ports).
  • PoE budget per switch: add up device watts from datasheets (or class maximums to be conservative) and compare with the switch's PoE budget (Lesson 12).
  • VLAN and IP plan: VLANs for staff, CCTV, guest Wi-Fi, management, voice. Subnets sized with Lesson 10's method, each with at least 25% spare addresses. Gateways on the first usable address.
  • lab onlyPacket Tracer model: at least the MDF plus one IDF, all VLANs, router-on-a-stick or a layer-3 switch, and ping tests (Lessons 12–13).
  • Rack elevation for the MDF (what sits in which U) and a one-line power diagram for the rack: UPS size, A/B feeds where possible (Lesson 14).
  • A one-page summary: assumptions, what you'd improve, and the bill of quantities. You're an estimator: include rough costs from real supplier price lists. Very few network candidates can do this.

Part 3 · Worked example (yours will differ)

Floor 2: 40 drops, 4 APs at 25.5 W, 10 cameras at 7 W.

  • Ports: 54 × 1.2 = 64.8 → 65 ports → one 48-port + one 24-port PoE switch (72 ports).
  • PoE: 4 × 25.5 + 10 × 7 = 102 + 70 = 172 W. Against a 370 W budget = 46% used. Comfortable.
  • UPS for the MDF rack (rule of thumb): add up all equipment watts, divide by a 0.9 power factor to get VA, then add 25% headroom. 1,276 W ÷ 0.9 ≈ 1,418 VA × 1.25 ≈ 1,772 VA → choose a 2 kVA or 3 kVA UPS.

Practice 1 · Check yourself

PASS/FAIL CHECK

  • Every drop is within 90 m of its telecom room (show the longest run).
  • Every switch has ≥ 20% spare ports and uses ≤ 80% of its PoE budget.
  • Every subnet has ≥ 25% spare addresses, and no two subnets overlap (check with the Lesson 10 Python command).
  • lab onlyIn Packet Tracer, a ping matrix (like Lesson 12's) matches your security intent: guest can't reach CCTV (use Lesson 21's ACL).
  • The MDF rack's UPS covers the full load with headroom, and every single-corded device is marked.
  • One practitioner (network engineer, data center tech or ELV engineer) has reviewed it, and you've written down their feedback. Post a redacted version on r/networking or r/homelab, or send it to someone on LinkedIn.

How you'll use it

  • Resume “Projects” section: “Designed the network for a 3-storey commercial building from electrical takeoffs: 162 ports, 5 VLANs, PoE and UPS sizing, verified in Packet Tracer.” (Use your real numbers.)
  • Interview: bring the one-page summary and the rack drawing. Walk through one decision (why that IDF location).
