SHEET 2

What an ACL is

read 8 min · predict 4 × 5 XP

An ACL (access control list) is an ordered list of permit and deny rules on a router. Each line says where traffic comes from, where it's going, and sometimes which protocol. In the village: the guard at the road junction now has a clipboard that says things like “Guest Street may not visit Staff Street.”

1

Top to bottom, first match wins

The router stops reading at the first matching line. A permit ip any any on top makes everything under it useless.

2

Invisible deny-all at the end

If no line matches, the packet is dropped. An ACL with only deny lines blocks all traffic.

3

One per interface, per direction

in = packets entering the router on that port. out = packets leaving on it.

Predict the guard

This ACL sits inbound on the guest door, g0/0.30. For each visitor, say what happens. The matching line lights up after you answer.

Standard vs extended. A standard ACL (1–99) checks only the source address, so place it close to the destination. An extended ACL (100–199, or named) checks source, destination and protocol, so place it close to the source and drop bad traffic before it travels. Our job, “guests may not reach staff, CCTV or the servers,” needs destinations: extended, inbound on g0/0.30.
SHEET 3

Wildcard forge

8 rounds · 10 XP each

ACLs don't use subnet masks. They use wildcard masks: 0 = must match, 1 = don't care. Shortcut: wildcard = 255.255.255.255 − mask. A /26 mask is 255.255.255.192, so its wildcard is 0.0.0.63. host 10.30.0.10 means wildcard 0.0.0.0, and any means 255.255.255.255.

SHEET 5

Lock the guard's own office

8 weak lines × 15 XP · SSH build 40 XP

The ACL protects the streets. But whoever logs into the router can delete the ACL, so the router needs protecting too. That's hardening: closing the easy ways in. Electricians know this idea already: it's LOTO and locked panel boards. Live parts stay behind a lock, and only named people hold a key.
