Switches and VLANs: hallways and locked doors

Every office, mall and data center network is built from switches and VLANs. You'll split one switch into three locked hallways, then size the PoE budget the way an estimator would.

≈ 60 min, half in Packet TracerLesson 10CCNA 2.1–2.2 · Network+ 2.0Cisco Packet Tracer
Why this lesson

NOC ads list VLANs (Job Ads #39); network technician and junior network engineer roles (Job Ads #34–36, #41–42) assume them. Packet Tracer is free from Cisco Networking Academy: sign up at netacad.com, then download.

Warm-up

Part 1 · What a switch does

A switch is the box in the comms room with many network ports. It connects devices on the same street. Every network card has a MAC address: a permanent ID burned in at the factory (like a unit owner's ID card), written like 00:1A:2B:3C:4D:5E.

The switch learns which MAC address lives behind which port, and keeps a MAC address table. When data arrives for a MAC it knows, it sends it out only that port. When it doesn't know, it floods it out of every port (like a broadcast).

Condo comparison: the switch is one floor's hallway. Everyone on that floor can knock on each other's doors.

Part 2 · VLANs: locked doors in the hallway

Problem: staff PCs, CCTV and guests are all plugged into the same switch. Without separation, a guest could reach the camera recorder.

A VLAN (Virtual LAN) splits one physical switch into separate virtual switches. Each port is assigned to one VLAN. Devices in VLAN 10 can't talk to devices in VLAN 20 at all, unless a router lets them (Lesson 13). Condo comparison: one physical hallway with locked doors, so it acts like separate hallways.

  • Access port: belongs to one VLAN. Used for PCs, cameras, phones.
  • Trunk port: carries many VLANs over one cable, between switches or to a router. Each frame gets a small VLAN tag (the 802.1Q standard) saying which VLAN it belongs to.

Part 3 · PoE: where your electrical knowledge pays off

Cameras, Wi-Fi access points and IP phones usually get their power through the network cable: PoE (Power over Ethernet). The switch is the power source, so every PoE switch has a power budget in watts.

StandardMax power at the switch port
802.3af (PoE)15.4 W
802.3at (PoE+)30 W
802.3bt (PoE++)60 W (Type 3) or 90 W (Type 4)

Your estimator brain already does this: add up the loads, compare with the supply, leave headroom. 30 cameras at about 7 W each = 210 W. A switch with a 370 W PoE budget handles that with room to spare. Most network people learn this the hard way. You can say it in an interview on day one.

Part 4 · Packet Tracer lab: build the office switch

30 minutes. One 2960 switch, six PCs. PC1–PC2 = staff, PC3–PC4 = CCTV, PC5–PC6 = guest.

  • lab onlyConnect PC1–PC6 to switch ports Fa0/1–Fa0/6 with copper straight-through cables.
  • lab onlyGive ALL six PCs addresses on the SAME test street first: 192.168.1.1 to 192.168.1.6, mask 255.255.255.0.
  • lab onlyPing from PC1 to PC3 and PC5. Both work: one big hallway, no locked doors.
